Why this addendum exists

Four sections (market, memory landscape, infra landscape, playbook) repeat one sentence: six memory-layer startups have raised about $51M between them, and single rounds at OpenRouter, LangChain, Glean, Braintrust and Contextual AI each exceed that total. The OpenRouter row came from a page that returned 403, the Qdrant row carried two contradictory round labels, the Portkey exit was a headline, the LangChain total did not reconcile with its listed rounds, and nobody had searched for a 2026 Mem0 or Letta follow-on. Any one of those could move the argument. The session's search budget was already exhausted when this pass began, so verification used eight direct fetches of primary pages (company blogs and TechCrunch), which is narrower than a search-backed check and is reflected in the confidence rating.

The sensitivity that matters is an unannounced Mem0 Series B. Mem0's own numbers (API calls from 35M to 186M a quarter across 2025, exclusive memory provider for AWS's Agent SDK) make a 2026 round plausible, and its blog publishes several times a week, so a round would normally surface there as it did in October 2025. That it has not is reasonable but not conclusive evidence: companies occasionally announce only through press or a filing. The Six Point Ventures note of July 22, 2026 still lists Mem0 at $24.5M and Letta at its $10M seed, which is the most recent third-party check available. Letta's 2026 output is product, not capital: an Agents SDK, a self-improvement mechanism and a coding app.

Arithmetic rather than estimate: the six companies would need a further $62M of follow-on to match OpenRouter's single Series B, and even the upside case with Plastic Labs is $47M short. The "seed-to-A category" label therefore holds as of today, with the explicit caveat that one search per company should be re-run when budget returns.

What changed since the sweep: the OpenRouter exit

The same TechCrunch check surfaced something the report does not yet contain. On August 16, 2026 TechCrunch reported that Stripe will acquire OpenRouter for $7B+, followed by an August 19 analysis piece on the rationale. This is reported, not closed, and no terms beyond the headline price were fetched. If it completes, it is the first large exit in the context-management stack and it lands in the gateway layer, about three months after a $1.3B post-money round. For the capital argument the comparison shifts from "a single round exceeds the memory category" to "a single reported exit is roughly 140 times the memory category's disclosed funding". For the incumbents section it is direct evidence that the gateway is where the metering and billing seat sits, which is consistent with the report's view that gateways commoditise model access rather than context.

Implications for a new entrant

  • The memory layer is still financed at seed-to-A scale ($51M across six companies, none with a disclosed 2026 growth round), so a new entrant is not competing against war chests and can win on product rather than spend.
  • Capital concentrates where usage is metered: OpenRouter's 100T tokens a month drew a $113M round and a reported $7B+ bid, which argues for building the memory or context product on top of a billing seat you can measure, not beside it.
  • Mem0's silence on funding despite 30% monthly API growth in 2025 is the single row to re-check before any investor conversation that relies on this report's numbers.
  • Plastic Labs' $15.4M Form D shows that follow-on capital for memory exists but is arriving quietly through filings, so track SEC Form D digests, not only press.
  • Treat every third-party funding snippet with a round label as suspect until the company's own post confirms it; Qdrant's $50M carries two labels and two dates in the same result set.
