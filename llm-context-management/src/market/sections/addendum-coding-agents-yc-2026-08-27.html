Addendum (Aug 27): coding-agent context products and the YC memory pipeline

Coding agents are where context management already monetizes: Cursor ($2.3B at $29.3B in Nov 2025, $50B talks by Apr 2026) bought open-source Continue in June 2026; Cognition raised $1B at $25B pre-money; Augment unbundled its Context Engine for any agent. The YC pipeline keeps feeding the layer; the standalone memory startups had no major news Aug 24 to 27.

The buy side: coding-agent majors are absorbing the context layer

Cursor (Anysphere) closed a $2.3B Series D at a $29.3B valuation on 13 Nov 2025 (per headline, CNBC), and by 17 Apr 2026 was in $2B+ talks at $50B after reaching roughly $2B ARR in three years (per headlines, TechCrunch and The Next Web). Its clearest context move: on 22 Jun 2026 The New Stack reported Cursor "quietly acquires Continue"; continue.dev confirms first-hand: "Continue has joined Cursor", with "our open-source codebase remains freely available". Continue pitched shareable custom assistants — portable context — now folded into the leader; terms unknown.

Cognition raised $1B at a $25B pre-money valuation on 27 May 2026 (per headline, TechCrunch), doubling its valuation post-Windsurf. Its site sells Devin plus "Devin in Windsurf", listing Goldman Sachs, Mercedes-Benz and Nubank among deployments; founder Scott Wu claims Devin writes 95 percent of Cognition's own code (per headline, Jul 2026). The fetched page leads with autonomy ("plans, writes, tests, and ships production code on its own"), not memory.

The sell side: context engines as standalone product

Augment Code made the report's key move: on 6 Feb 2026 its Context Engine became available "for any AI coding agent" (per headline, Augment blog) — retrieval unbundled from the assistant. Its homepage (27 Aug 2026) now leads with Cosmos, "the platform for building an always-on software delivery system that moves work from spec to verification", with "shared context and memory" listed as a platform feature. Its funding is not restated on the site: unknown.

Amp, tied to Sourcegraph in earlier coverage, now brands itself "the frontier agent" — "a coding agent and a development environment", multi-model, and its docs productize shared context: Threads ("you can save and share your interactions with Amp"), Workspaces, enterprise thread-visibility controls and AGENTS.md customization. The docs no longer name Sourcegraph anywhere, and sourcegraph.com blocked fetching (HTTP 403); Amp-specific capital: unknown. Cline holds the open end: "The Open Coding Agent", Apache 2.0, 67k stars, 250+ contributors, "8M+ developers" and "8.0M+ installs", monetizing via enterprise controls and ClinePass; capital unknown (not on site, no headline found). Its 2026 news was security: a Kanban WebSocket flaw let malicious websites hijack the agent (per headlines, Infosecurity Magazine and gbhackers, May 2026).

YC pipeline and the memory layer since Aug 24

YC keeps feeding the layer, but a full W25 to S26 enumeration was again not possible within caps. Verified: Mastra (YC W25, $22M Series A in Apr 2026 per its YC page) bundles memory into a TypeScript agent framework; Confident AI (YC W25) ships DeepEval; Mem0 (YC alum) remains the funding benchmark at $24M (Oct 2025). Memory-tagged names inside the F25 to S26 batches remain unenumerated: unknown.

The memory layer proper was quiet in the Aug 24 to 27 window: across five news queries, nothing dated after 22 Aug 2026 surfaced for Mem0, Letta, Zep, Supermemory or MemTensor. Nearest signals: a Weaviate Engram versus Mem0 buyer's guide (per headline, Sangri Today, 22 Aug 2026 — big-vendor encroachment), and third-party ARR estimates of roughly $1.4M for Letta and $1M for Zep (per headlines, GetLatka, 10 Aug 2026; low reliability). The one material post-report event is Chinese: MemTensor closed a roughly 100M RMB pre-A on 24 Jul 2026 (per headline, 品玩), its second nine-figure-RMB round, while saying it is "not a memory company". Supermemory's $3M seed (Oct 2025, per TechCrunch headline) keeps being retold in mid-2026 press — visibility, not new capital.

Implications

  • The context layer is consolidating into coding-agent majors — Continue into Cursor, Windsurf into Cognition — the likeliest exit for standalone context startups is absorption by $25B-plus agents.
  • Augment's standalone Context Engine (Feb 2026) validates the report's context-as-sellable-infrastructure thesis, but it also parks a funded incumbent directly in the memory-API startups' lane.
  • Cline's 8M installs on undisclosed capital show the open-source runtime commoditizes agent scaffolding; value accrues to the context and data layer, and the May 2026 Kanban vulnerability will push enterprises toward managed context products.
  • A quiet Aug 24 to 27 window for the five memory-layer startups means no re-rating since the report; MemTensor's pre-A leaves the China track funded ahead of most US seed peers.
  • Coverage debt remains: the YC F25 to S26 memory enumeration, Amp and Augment capital positions, and Cline funding are unknown — table blanks are not zeros.
