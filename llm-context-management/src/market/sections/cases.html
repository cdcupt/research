Reference startups: how they started and what it teaches

Every durable entrant began with a narrow, free artifact (a paper, a library, a consumer tool) and converted attention into a hosted API 12 to 24 months later. Rounds were justified by usage (API calls, pipeline runs), not stars. The mistakes to avoid: vendor benchmarks that do not reproduce, infrastructure companies drifting into apps, and treating a flat launch as the end.

Mem0: a complaint from a side project became the default memory API

Taranjeet Singh and Deshraj Yadav had built Embedchain (8,000+ stars) and a meditation app whose users complained that "the app doesn't remember"; Mem0 launched in January 2024 (TechCrunch). The Show HN on 4 Sept 2024 drew 201 points, and commenters already raised today's objections: open-source commitment, PII handled by prompting, memory bloat. A $3.9M seed led by Kindred went unannounced; the $20M Series A led by Basis Set was disclosed on 28 Oct 2025 with API calls up from 35M (Q1 2025) to 186M (Q3 2025), 80,000+ cloud signups and AWS naming Mem0 the exclusive memory provider for its Agent SDK. By 20 July 2026 the repository had 61,323 stars (Mnemoverse).

What worked: a three-line integration, 25+ framework integrations and an MCP server as distribution. What did not: credibility. Mem0 reports 94.4% on LongMemEval; an independent reproduction (Maximem, 27 May 2026) measured 73.8%, and a February 2026 Ask HN from a YC founder called it "key-value memory with semantic search" that cannot learn preferences.

Letta: paper, then open source, then cloud, then a drift toward an app

The MemGPT paper (UC Berkeley) appeared on 12 Oct 2023. The repository was renamed Letta on 23 Sept 2024 and a $10M seed led by Felicis was announced the next day, with angels including Jeff Dean and Clem Delangue (press release); BigDATAwire reported a $70M post-money valuation. Then the centre of gravity moved: Letta Code launched in December 2025 (42.5% on Terminal-Bench), a desktop app in April 2026, LettaBot was folded into Letta Code in May 2026, and letta.com/pricing now redirects to Letta Code pricing (Ry Walker, Letta docs). At 23,887 stars in July 2026 it is the smallest repository of the five despite the earliest research.

The lesson: research credibility buys a seed and strong angels, but a runtime billed per active agent is a harder sell than a drop-in API, and the company followed usage into coding agents.

Zep: a flat launch, then a second act in temporal graphs and compliance

Daniel Chalef's Show HN on 10 May 2023 earned 7 points for an open-source memory store. Tracxn shows one disclosed $500K seed (April 2024) and 8 employees in May 2026. The second act is Graphiti, an Apache-2.0 temporal knowledge-graph engine backed by the "Zep: A Temporal Knowledge Graph Architecture for Agent Memory" paper (arXiv 2501.13956, January 2025); it reached 28,981 stars by July 2026 and 30.2k by August, more than Letta. Commercially Zep went up-market: Enterprise lists SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA BAA, BYOC and an EU DPA, Flex is credit-metered at $1,250 a year (Zep pricing), and AgentMarketCap calls it the only vendor with the full GDPR, SOC 2 and HIPAA set in 2026.

The lesson: a weak launch is survivable, an engine can out-star its company, and compliance is a wedge a small team can own while better-funded rivals gate it behind "contact sales".

Supermemory: consumer pull converted into a developer API

Dhravya Shah started Supermemory in summer 2024 as a consumer open-source "second brain"; it reached 10,000 stars and 50,000+ users, and companies asked for the underlying infrastructure. A $3M seed led by Susa Ventures with Browder Capital and SF1.vc was announced on 6 Oct 2025 (press reported $2.6M), with angels including Cloudflare CTO Dane Knecht and Sentry's David Cramer (company blog). The repository stood at 28,509 stars in July 2026, MIT-licensed clients around a closed engine; a free single-machine local binary shipped in June 2026. Pricing is token-metered from $19 to $399 a month with connectors as tier differentiators (pricing).

The lesson: a consumer tool is a cheap discovery channel for infrastructure demand, and open clients around a closed engine trade developer trust for defensibility.

Cognee: a later, graph-first, enterprise entrant from Berlin

Founded in 2024 by Vasilije Markovic, Cognee sells an Extract-Cognify-Load pipeline that turns 38+ sources into a knowledge graph. Pipeline runs grew from about 2,000 to over 1 million during 2025; it is live in 70+ companies including Bayer and the University of Wyoming; and it had 12,000+ stars and 80+ contributors when the $7.5M seed led by Pebblebed (Pamela Vagata, Keith Adams) with 42CAP and Vermilion was announced on 19 Feb 2026 (Cognee). Stars reached 28,786 by July 2026. Cognee publishes no LoCoMo or LongMemEval score.

The lesson: entering two years after Mem0 still produced a large seed by picking the structured, regulated-enterprise lane and reporting a usage metric (pipeline runs) investors accepted.

LangChain (adjacent): the framework that bundles memory as a feature

LangChain raised a $10M seed led by Benchmark (April 2023), a $25M Series A led by Sequoia at $200M (February 2024) and a $125M Series B led by IVP at $1.25B on 20 Oct 2025, with strategic backers including ServiceNow, Datadog and Databricks (LangChain, SiliconANGLE). It cites 90M monthly downloads, 35% of the Fortune 500 and a 12x rise in LangSmith trace volume. The library is free; the business is observability and evals. LangMem is its framework-native memory.

The lesson: the framework will bundle memory, so either become the default integration inside it (Mem0's approach) or be something a framework cannot be: a compliance-grade store, a temporal graph, a runtime.

Implications for a new entrant

  • Ship a free artifact first and expect 12 to 24 months before a priced round: Mem0, Supermemory and Cognee all followed that lag.
  • Report usage, not stars: the rounds cited API calls per quarter (Mem0) and pipeline runs (Cognee), while the team that led with research (Letta) has the fewest stars today.
  • Do not lead with a vendor benchmark nobody has reproduced; Mem0's 94.4% against Maximem's 73.8% is now quoted in every comparison.
  • Pick a billing unit that matches the buyer: requests (Mem0), credits (Zep), active agents (Letta) or tokens (Supermemory) each signal a different customer.
  • Compliance (SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA BAA, BYOC, EU DPA) is a wedge an 8-person company owns today; regulated buyers pay for it before they pay for benchmark points.
  • Assume frameworks and platforms will bundle memory (LangMem, Cloudflare, Claude Code auto-memory); be the default integration inside them or be structurally different.
