Private markets: funding trends and what investors expect

AI absorbed 80% of an all-time-record venture market in early 2026, with two labs — OpenAI and Anthropic — alone taking 43% of every startup dollar in H1. Investors are paying steep premiums for that exposure while exits only partially reopen, and the loudest theses on where the next non-lab dollars go converge on one layer: agent memory, context, and harness infrastructure.

Record dollars, unprecedented concentration

Global venture funding hit a record $510B in H1 2026 — more than the $440B invested in all of 2025 — per Crunchbase (Jul 2, 2026). Q1 alone was $305B, the largest quarter ever recorded; Q2 added $205B across 5,000+ startups. The composition is the story: AI startups captured 80% of global venture funding in Q1 2026, up from roughly 50% through 2025 (AI first crossed 50% in Q4 2024), per Crunchbase's concentration analysis (Apr 16, 2026). Four rounds — OpenAI $122B, Anthropic $30B (initial tranche), xAI $20B, Waymo $16B — totaled $188B, nearly 65% of all global venture investment that quarter. This is bigger checks, not more deals: North American dollars surged 190% y/y while deal count fell 26%. OpenAI and Anthropic together took $217B, 43% of all H1 2026 startup funding. Pricing followed: PitchBook data (May 18, 2026) shows Q1 2026 Series A AI companies pricing at an 84% premium to non-AI peers (Series B: 55%, up from 39% in 2025), while AI-related companies now make up half of total VC market valuation and US dry powder has fallen to $278.5B from its $323.3B 2023 peak.

Mega-rounds: lab valuations outrun the asset class

The largest private rounds in history all closed within months of each other. Anthropic's Series H (May 28, 2026) — $65B at $965B post-money, led by Altimeter, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, and Sequoia with $15B from hyperscalers including $5B from Amazon — was announced alongside a $47B revenue run-rate; Reuters reported that run rate topping $65B by Aug 17, 2026 (per headline). Business Insider reported Anthropic trading as high as $1.5T on secondary markets by Aug 14, 2026 — up 25% in a month, with almost no sellers (per headline; page blocked). China's parallel wave is smaller but steep: Moonshot AI (月之暗面) raised ~$2B led by Meituan Longzhu (美团龙珠) at >$20B post-money on May 7, 2026 — four rounds totaling >$3.9B in under six months, per 21财经 — while Zhipu (智谱) and MiniMax already trade in Hong Kong.

Agent and context infrastructure: where the non-lab theses point

The most specific 2026 VC theses target the layer this report studies. Bessemer's AI Infrastructure Roadmap (Mar 30, 2026) names harness infrastructure, continual learning, RL platforms, the inference inflection, and world models as its five frontiers — arguing "most enterprise AI systems suffer from organizational amnesia," that the gap is "orchestrating components like knowledge retrieval, cross-session context management, and planning," and that as models commoditize, advantage shifts to the memory layer. It estimates 78% of AI failures are invisible, naming Braintrust, Judgment Labs, and Bigspin.ai in evals/observability. Y Combinator's Fall 2026 RFS asks directly for multiplayer agent sessions ("anyone on a team should be able to drop into the same live agent session"), self-maintaining APIs applied by coding agents, and a cloud for agent-built software. Actual context-layer rounds are still seed-to-A scale: Mem0 raised $24M ($3.9M seed + $20M Series A led by Basis Set, with YC, Peak XV, and GitHub Fund) to build a memory layer for AI apps — API calls grew from 35M in Q1 2025 to 186M in Q3 2025, and it is the exclusive memory provider for AWS's Agent SDK, per TechCrunch (Oct 28, 2025). Cognee raised €7.5M for enterprise-grade agent memory (EU-Startups, Feb 19, 2026, per headline) and TechCrunch covered Reload's shared agent memory (Feb 19, 2026, per headline). Demand-side support: Menlo Ventures put 2025 enterprise genAI investment at $37B, tripling in one year (Dec 9, 2025).

Exits: from drought to a selective reopening

Q1 2026 still looked like a liquidity drought — only 15 VC-backed IPOs, a pace PitchBook called "well short" of clearing the backlog, with rolling one-year VC IRR at 14.6% (Q3 2025). Q2 turned: SpaceX went public at $1.77T raising $75B; Anysphere was acquired for $60B, the largest startup acquisition ever; 32 companies IPO'd above $1B; and 24 companies were acquired at $1B+ totaling $113B — the highest quarter on record, per Crunchbase. But breadth is thin: only 3 of 13 companies in the 2025 tech IPO class were above water by Feb 24, 2026 (SaaStr, per headline), and mid-size AI M&A clears at modest prices — Okta bought Permiso for ~$200M (TechCrunch, Jul 30, 2026); Cisco bought Astrix for $400M (May 4, 2026, per headline). The pivotal event is Anthropic's IPO: filed June 2026, with Fortune reporting a floated ~$2T valuation it says requires "Amazon-style earnings" to justify (Aug 14, 2026, per headline). In China, Zhipu's HK market cap crossed ~¥1T by end-June 2026 (华尔街见闻, per headline) while MiniMax deflated ahead of its first lockup expiry (21财经, Jun 22, 2026, per headline).

The skeptic case — and the context-infrastructure rebuttal

The canonical skeptic datapoint is MIT NANDA's GenAI Divide report (Aug 2025): ~95% of enterprise genAI pilots deliver no measurable P&L impact (150 interviews, 350 surveyed employees, 300 deployments). Official-sector warnings stacked up in Aug 2026: Tangen, ECB economists flagging 2026 correction risk (Aug 19, per headline), BofA's derivatives desk seeing bubble indicators near dot-com extremes (华尔街见闻, Aug 7, per headline), and Dudley's end-2027 burst call. The rebuttals are twofold. First, MIT itself located the failure in tools that "don't learn from or adapt to workflows" — a context-and-memory gap, not a model-quality gap — which is precisely the layer Bessemer, YC, and the Mem0/Cognee cohort are funding. Second, revenue is arriving faster than skeptics modeled: Anthropic's run rate went from $47B (May 28) to a reported >$65B (Aug 17, per Reuters headline), Menlo measured enterprise spend tripling to $37B, and JPMorgan raised its S&P 500 target on evidence AI capex is "finally delivering" (Business Insider, Aug 10, 2026, per headline). The unresolved question is price, not demand: an 84% entry premium plus $278.5B of shrinking dry powder leaves little room for multiple compression.

Implications

  • Context/memory infrastructure is the consensus non-lab thesis for 2026 — Bessemer, YC, and Sequoia all point at the harness/memory layer, yet actual rounds there (Mem0 $24M, Cognee €7.5M) are 3-4 orders of magnitude smaller than lab rounds, leaving room for fast markups.
  • Two companies now set the clearing price for the entire asset class: OpenAI and Anthropic took 43% of H1 2026 funding, and PitchBook warns their IPOs could absorb enough capital to push broader VC recovery to 2027.
  • The MIT 95% pilot-failure figure functions as a bull argument for this report's subject: the diagnosed cause (no learning, no retained context) is the product gap context-infra startups sell against.
  • Entry prices embed the thesis: 84% Series A AI premiums (Q1 2026) mean context-infra investors need either lab-scale outcomes or fast strategic M&A (Okta/Permiso at ~$200M shows the realistic mid-case).
  • Watch Anthropic's IPO (filed Jun 2026 per headline, ~$2T floated) as the repricing event for every private AI mark, US and China alike — secondaries at ~$1.5T already price in a successful debut.
