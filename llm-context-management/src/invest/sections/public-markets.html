The stock situation: AI in public markets

Into late August 2026, public-market AI runs a split screen: record index levels and rising strategist targets on one side; the world's largest sovereign fund, ECB economists and a former Fed vice-chair calling a bubble on the other. Capex is now punished rather than rewarded, mega-cap concentration reached ~40% by March 2026, and the whole trade compresses into Nvidia's August 26 print.

The bubble debate now has named voices

The warning side escalated sharply in August. Norway's sovereign wealth fund reported a record NOK 1,753B (~$184B) return driven by the AI rally while saying it cannot hedge against a reversal (Tech Times, Aug 12), and CEO Nicolai Tangen followed with an explicit AI-bubble alarm (DW, Aug 18) that Yahoo Finance framed as “crying wolf” after record profit (Aug 17). ECB economists flagged AI-bubble and US tech-correction risk for 2026 (Intellectia, Aug 19). Ex–NY Fed president Bill Dudley (比尔·达德利) said the AI investment boom has peaked and the bubble may burst before end-2027 (新浪财经, Aug 24, per headline). BofA's derivatives desk put its AI-bubble risk indicator near dot-com extremes (华尔街见闻, Aug 7); Jim Rogers said he liquidated US stocks entirely while keeping China (星岛頭條, Aug 16). The nuanced middle: one strategist calls it a “rolling sequence of bubbles” across AI sub-sectors (Fortune, Aug 16). Bulls kept raising: Citi lifted its S&P 500 year-end target to 8,100 on an AI “supercycle” (Reuters, Jun 8), and JPMorgan bumped its target in August arguing AI capex is finally delivering earnings (Business Insider, Aug 10). July CPI cooling to 3.4% (Benzinga, Aug 12) kept the rate-cut bid under the rally.

Nvidia, semis and the August 26 referendum

The market treats Nvidia's Aug 26 report as the referendum on the whole AI-spending boom (24/7 Wall St., Aug 21). Setup, per TradingKey (Aug 19–23 headlines): Q1 FY27 revenue $81.6B with data-center up 92% y/y, $91.0B ±2% guided for Q2 (Nvidia release, May 20, 2026), the Vera Rubin platform ramping, and Nvidia tied to financing a $500B AI fund. The tape is twitchy: the stock fell after earnings four straight quarters despite beats, options imply ~$316B of market cap at stake (TechStock2, Aug 21), and ~$562B of cap was added in early August alone (Aug 10). China is the hole in the story: a US Commerce official confirmed H200 sales to China at zero with approvals stalled (电子工程专辑, Feb 25), and Chinese media describe Nvidia's China AI-chip share going from ~95% to ~0% (36Kr, May 8). China-exposure risk bites hard when it lands: Super Micro fell 33% in a day after employees were charged with smuggling Nvidia chips (CNBC, Mar 19). The domestic-substitution winner trades in Shanghai: Cambricon (寒武纪, 688256.SS) peaked at a ¥1T (~$140B) market cap on Jun 30, 2026, then fell below ¥700B (~$97B) by Aug 13, 2026, and guides to $14B revenue over three years (SCMP, Jul 29).

Hyperscaler capex: the reaction function flipped

Cumulative Big Tech AI spending passed $1tn (FT, Jul 30), with 2026 combined capex for Alphabet, Microsoft, Meta and Amazon estimated at $725B, up 77% y/y (Tom's Hardware, Apr 30) and later raised to $760B (Statista, Jul 31, per headline). The regime change: capex now outruns operating cash flow, forcing external financing (FactSet, Jul 23; Reuters on FCF pressure, Jul 22) — and the market started punishing it. Alphabet's late-July report sparked an AI-capex selloff that turned investors skeptical into the Amazon/Meta/Microsoft prints (CNBC, Jul 28); cloud revenue growth divided winners from losers in the early-August selloff (TechStock2, Aug 2). The same discipline reached Hong Kong on Aug 24: Alibaba — up ~34% on its Qwen-led AI strategy into Aug 19 — priced a US$10.2B (HK$80B) placement Aug 23–24, 2026 to fund AI spending and triggered a selloff that dropped the Hang Seng below 25,500 — HK shares fell 8–9.8%, while Tsai/Wu bought HK$120M into the dip (finance.biggo, Aug 24).

Concentration risk

The Magnificent Seven's grip on the S&P 500 reached roughly 40% of index weight, a record framed as a systemic alarm (Chronicle-Journal, Mar 9); early-August coverage pegged AI-linked S&P concentration as a “$27 trillion” issue (finance.biggo, Aug 6; Motley Fool, Aug 8). Norway's fund is the institutional embodiment: index-constrained, it says it cannot hedge the very rally that produced its NOK 1,753B (~$184B) record. For allocators, passive exposure is now an AI bet whether chosen or not (Fortune's “nowhere to hide,” Feb 4).

AI IPOs: two waves, one lesson

Wave one (US, 2025 class incl. CoreWeave, Figma, Circle, Navan): only 3 of 13 traded above IPO price by Feb 24 (SaaStr, Feb 2026). CoreWeave remains the AI-infra bellwether: Q2 revenue roughly doubled y/y and the stock popped ~14%, though losses widened (CNBC, Aug 11). Wave two runs through Hong Kong: Zhipu (2513.HK) listed Jan 7 after a $560M raise and climbed; MiniMax (MINIMAX-W) doubled on debut Jan 9, ran ~400% by end-May, then lost ~$39B of market cap in July's record lock-up-expiry wave (Forbes, Jul 6) — and still rallied 11% on an open-source model launch (per 新浪财经 headline, Aug 22). Moonshot AI (月之暗面) completed shareholding reform on a $35B valuation while raising pre-IPO money at $50B talk (Sina, Aug 21; 财联社, Aug 5). The 2026 US pipeline still dangles SpaceX, Anthropic and OpenAI (Barron's, Jan 2). Software incumbents, meanwhile, repriced upward: Datadog jumped 31% on AI-lab-assisted earnings (SiliconANGLE, May 7) and Truist hiked Snowflake and MongoDB targets on Aug 20.

What confirms or breaks the trade

  • Confirm: Nvidia Aug 26 — gross margin holding and a clean Vera Rubin ramp against the $91.0B guide; a fifth straight post-earnings drop would be the tell that beats no longer clear expectations (TradingKey, Aug 23).
  • Confirm: Q3 hyperscaler prints showing cloud/AI revenue growing into the $725–760B capex — the JPMorgan “capex is delivering” thesis surviving contact with guidance (Aug 10).
  • Break: capex guided up again while FCF compresses and more debt/placements appear — the Alibaba Aug 24 placement selloff is the template for how funding supply now hits prices.
  • Break: concentration unwinding — any forced de-rating of a ~40%-weight Mag 7 drags every index product with it; watch whether breadth improves on rallies (CME, Aug 10).
  • Swing factor: rates — July CPI at 3.4% keeps the cut path alive; a hawkish surprise removes the rally's floor (Aug 12).

Implications

  • Plan for funding-cost reality, not narrative: even hyperscalers now finance AI capex externally — downstream, expect tighter scrutiny of any AI budget line that lacks revenue attach.
  • Nvidia's Aug 26 print is a genuine event risk for private AI valuations too; term sheets signed the week after will reprice with it.
  • Capex announcements no longer buy stock-price credit (Alphabet Jul 28, Alibaba Aug 24); if you sell to hyperscalers, budget approvals will demand ROI evidence sooner.
  • The IPO window is open but unforgiving: 3-of-13 above water as of Feb 24, 2026, and HK lock-up expiries erased ~$39B from MiniMax — sequence any listing plans around supply overhangs.
  • Concentration means beta is an AI bet: operator treasuries and employee equity heavily indexed to the S&P are implicitly long the same trade as the product roadmap.
  • China runs a parallel market with its own cycle (Cambricon ¥1T peak Jun 30 → below ¥700B by Aug 13, 2026, Zhipu/MiniMax listings, state capital) — US-listed exposure to it now carries enforcement risk, per SMCI's –33% day.
